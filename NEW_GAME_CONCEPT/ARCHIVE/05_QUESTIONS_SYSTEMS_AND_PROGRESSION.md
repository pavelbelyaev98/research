# 05 — Questions, Round 3: Systems and Progression

Rules and variations only — exact numbers are prototype-tuned (Rule 8). Answer by ID in chat; answers
are recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.

This round covers what money buys, how levels feel, and how the pressure systems behave. Content and
discoveries (roster, detector targets, mystery beats) is Round 4; interface/accessibility Round 5.

---

### S01 — Which upgrade tracks exist?
**Context:** Each track should matter in every zone, and no single track should dominate the run.
The tool itself has one shared upgrade level (Q30f).
**Options:**
- **A)** Five tracks: Dig Tool, Battery, Jetpack, Inventory, Detector. C4 is consumable-only.
- **B)** Five tracks + a small C4 track (blast and pack upgrades, few levels). *(Recommended)*
- **C)** B + one or two "strange late-game" tracks (mystery upgrades).
- **D)** Three tracks only (tool, battery, inventory).
**Recommendation:** B. Six tracks spread purchases across the run (fixing the "maxed everything
halfway" failure), and C4 deserves progression if it is a headline late tool. Mystery tracks are
flavor for later, not scope for 1.0.
> **ANSWER:**

### S02 — Level structure and milestones
**Context:** Some levels should transform capability; others can be satisfying increments. Players
disliked both invisible stat boosts and endless padding tiers.
**Options:**
- **A)** Six levels per track. Pattern: increments with a **major capability change every ~third
  level** (new shape, new access, dramatic efficiency). *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Every level is a major transformation — wonderful in theory, very hard to design across all
  tracks without power creep.
- **C)** Continuous stat ranks (no distinct tiers) — cheap, but produces no memorable moments.
**Recommendation:** A. `[MELT]`'s visible bolt-on upgrades were praised; `[OMT]`'s "+5% invisible"
upgrades were hated; `[SPOT]` felt like late tiers were padding. Increments with punctuation.
> **ANSWER:**

### S03 — Economy rules
**Context:** Money is the only progression currency. The failure modes to avoid: dead ends where
everything is bought, RNG gates, and multi-currency management.
**Options:**
- **A)** Money only; one level at a time; every purchase shows current→next effect; final meaningful
  purchase arrives near the end of the run; unlimited small sinks late (lamps, charges, display
  pieces, cosmetics). *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Add a second late-game currency (e.g., core shards) for top tiers.
- **C)** Allow rare finds to unlock purchases directly (find-to-buy shortcuts).
**Recommendation:** A. RNG/blueprint gates are among the most-hated mechanics in the reference set;
multi-currency violates the NOT-list; late sinks solve `[AGADH]`/`[MELT]`'s "nothing left to buy".
> **ANSWER:**

### S04 — Fuel rules
**Context:** The shared battery creates the core "do I keep digging?" decision. The failure mode to
avoid: a battery that destroys loot or forces constant commuting.
**Options:**
- **A)** Shared dig+jetpack battery; refill purchased at the surface (full or partial, price shown);
  drain only on powered actions; return-power warning with safe/risky/critical states; bigger tanks
  keep current fuel rather than refilling. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Refill is free on return (removes the money sink and most of the tension).
- **C)** Fuel capsules found underground (adds carry-management and RNG).
**Recommendation:** A. `[AGADH]`'s battery was hated for destroying loot, not for existing; recover
handles failure with a fee instead of deletion (`Q26`), so the tension stays and the punishment goes.
> **ANSWER:**

### S05 — Capacity rules
**Context:** Inventory pressure should end trips, never destroy finds. The return decision should
come from battery + a full bag, in that order.
**Options:**
- **A)** Starting capacity supports a satisfying first trip before upgrades; capacity grows in
  strong steps; overflow is auto-sent to surface storage (never deleted); uniques never consume
  capacity. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Tight starting capacity to teach management (risks the "dig 5 s, walk 30 s" failure).
- **C)** Unlimited inventory (battery becomes the only reason to surface).
**Recommendation:** A. `[AGADH]` inventory overflow destroyed ore and `[OMT]`'s rare slots deleted
items; both are explicit anti-patterns here. Storage-overflow keeps pressure without theft.
> **ANSWER:**

### S06 — Detector progression
**Context:** The detector is the curiosity engine. It must create "what's over there?" without
becoming a treasure GPS or making ordinary finds obsolete.
**Options:**
- **A)** Upgrades add range, cue clarity and later broad direction; cues never reveal value or
  rarity; silent (visual) presentation; one foreground target at a time; quiet intervals between
  cues. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Add optional audio ticks (must remain fully playable muted).
- **C)** Add value/rarity tiers to cues.
**Recommendation:** A. C is explicitly rejected: a "special cue" makes everything else feel like
waste. B is acceptable only as an addition to A, never the primary channel.
> **ANSWER:**

### S07 — Jetpack progression
**Context:** Movement progression is a second power fantasy alongside digging, and it directly shapes
how return trips feel.
**Options:**
- **A)** Every level noticeably better; milestones change capability (boost, steering, sustained
  ascent) rather than percentages; stays controllable in narrow player-made shafts; wall bumps never
  damaging. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Numbers only (fuel cost, thrust).
- **C)** Three big tiers instead of many small ones.
**Recommendation:** A. `[SPOT]`'s slow-recharging jetpack and `[OMT]`'s rope-only traversal show how
much movement quality matters to how the game feels in hour two versus hour five.
> **ANSWER:**

### S08 — C4 rules
**Context:** C4 exists to be dramatic: a placed, remotely detonated, powerful hole-maker. The failure
mode is the "trap upgrade" that costs a lot and clears little.
**Options:**
- **A)** Placed, remote-detonated; forgiving snap placement with clear valid/invalid preview;
  substantial predictable blast with matching cleanup; charges cost money; an optional accelerator,
  never the only way past anything. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Throwable dynamite that bounces and rolls.
- **C)** Free charges on a cooldown.
**Recommendation:** A. `[KD]`'s dynamite was a named trap ("bombs do nothing"); `[AGADH]`'s dynamite
clipped through targets. C removes the economy decision that makes saving for explosions satisfying.
> **ANSWER:**

### S09 — Tool modes and attachments
**Context:** Shave and Scoop exist as different digging feels on the same machine; the late nozzle
covers the "make it absurd" escalation. The failure mode is mandatory mode-switching or invalidation.
**Options:**
- **A)** One machine, one shared upgrade level. Attachments unlock Shave (fast, precise), Scoop
  (wide, cheap) and late Nozzle (blast); switching is instant and optional; no material requires a
  specific mode. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Modes must be matched to materials (gating pattern).
- **C)** Scoop only; drop modes entirely.
**Recommendation:** A. `[MELT]`'s forced tool switch is the cautionary tale; `[AGADH]`'s strictly
better drill shows replacement can feel great when nothing is lost.
> **ANSWER:**

### S10 — Rare permanent passive upgrades
**Context:** A few buried finds can grant permanent benefits instead of money — optional, memorable,
never required.
**Options:**
- **A)** Three: a detector boost, a jetpack efficiency boost, a C4 efficiency boost. Unsellable,
  displayed, optional. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Four to five.
- **C)** None; only purchasable upgrades.
**Recommendation:** A. They give deep finds a story beyond money without threatening balance; `[MELT]`
artifacts that "gave no perks" were criticized, so each one must grant something tangible.
> **ANSWER:**

### S11 — Shop presentation
**Context:** The shop must always show a next goal and never feel like a spreadsheet or a mystery box.
**Options:**
- **A)** Every purchase shows current→next, cost and practical benefit; locked items are visible with
  their requirement; no unexplained icons; no story permission gates. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Surprise unlocks (hide what comes next).
- **C)** Raw stat tables.
**Recommendation:** A. `[AGADH]`'s shop was praised for simplicity, but its late game died from
having nothing left; visible future tiers solve that without adding complexity.
> **ANSWER:**

### S12 — After the ending
**Context:** The finale must not end the player's reason to play. Continue Playing is confirmed.
**Options:**
- **A)** After the cutscene: all remaining purchases unlock, the display can be completed, missed
  discoveries can be found, achievements stay obtainable on the same save; no reset required. *(Recommended)*
- **B)** Nothing left to do; the story is over.
- **C)** The game offers a fresh save only.
**Recommendation:** A. `[AGADH]` ended with an empty chest and disabled tools; `[SPOT]` had no
post-game. Continue Playing with a live economy is the cheapest, strongest fix.
> **ANSWER:**
