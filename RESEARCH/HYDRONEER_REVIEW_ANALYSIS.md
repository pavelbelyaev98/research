## TL;DR

Hydroneer is a first-person mining and base-building sandbox from Foulball Hangover, which began as a solo project by its founder Max. You start on an empty plot with a shovel, a bucket, a pan and a truck; you dig dirt, wash it for ore and gems, sell the results, and reinvest in water-powered drills, conveyors, harvesters, pipes and vehicles until your hole runs itself. Years of free updates added fishing, farming and cooking, NPC quests, achievements, a wire/logic system, creative mode, Workshop mods and split-screen co-op via Steam Remote Play; the paid DLC **Journey to Volcalidus** (April 2024) added a lava-and-glacier island, and the May 2026 Mega-Optimization patch coincided with the console launch.

The game sits at Very Positive overall, but sentiment has been sliding: 90.1% positive in 2021 versus 77.3% in 2026, with the worst months landing after sale waves that bring in new players (September 2024: 68.1%) and during the save-corruption era (March 2025: 75.2%).

- **What reviewers praise:** the dig → wash → sell → automate loop and the satisfaction of watching a factory you built actually run; the tactile "everything is a physical object" design that makes the game genuinely unique; water-powered, steampunk engineering and base building; the chill, no-pressure pace; the low price and the developer's transparency; split-screen co-op for couples and families.
- **What reviewers criticize:** the no-inventory, one-item-at-a-time handling that dominates the mid- and late-game chores; the drop-placement cursor and missing tutorial; physics chaos and item loss; years of save-corruption reports; performance collapse with large factories; the "Co-op" tag that means split-screen/Remote Play only; progression that runs out once the first automated mine prints money; quest and token grinds; and the feeling that the base game was declared finished so a paid DLC could ship.

## What reviewers praise (the good)

### 1. The dig → wash → sell → automate loop is hypnotic
The most common praise is the loop itself: shovel dirt, wash it in a pan, sell, upgrade, repeat — and then automate each step away. Reviewers describe losing days to it and discovering that the "boring" manual phase was secretly a tutorial for the factory they end up building.

> "Hydroneer is one of those games that looks simple at first, but slowly turns into something much deeper and more addictive than expected. At the start, it seems like a basic mining and crafting game where you dig dirt, wash it, sell resources, and upgrade your tools. But after playing for a while, you realize that the real fun comes from building your own systems, automating your work, improving your base, and slowly turning a tiny mining operation into a huge machine-powered money-making setup." — English, 40.5 h, positive, 29 upvotes
>
> "Not sure what happened. Got sick and two weeks later I have 200 hours played. Can't recommend this game enough." — English, 205.6 h, positive, 32 upvotes
>
> "Dig hole, life good. Hole dig back!?! Kill Hole! Hole gone... Think about hole. Regret." — English, 27.3 h, positive, 96 upvotes

### 2. Watching a factory you built actually run
Buying the first drill, then adding a harvester, a sorter and a smelter, and seeing the machine do the job you spent hours doing by hand is the emotional peak of the game for most positive reviewers — and the logic system gives tinkerers a long-term project.

> "This game has some insane potential. The automation system feels intuitive and fun. The selling and buying system is also intuitive and fun. Pretty much everything is intuitive and fun. I was able to make a fully functional automated mine without watching any tutorials." — English, 10.8 h, positive, 39 upvotes

### 4. Water-powered steampunk engineering
Hydroneer's machines run on water pressure delivered through player-built pipe networks — a system reviewers call novel and satisfying to lay out. The medieval/steampunk look and the ability to build bases, houses and production lines without size limits are recurring positives.

## What reviewers criticize (the bad)

### 1. No inventory: one item at a time is the defining complaint
The single most common criticism across eight years of reviews is the handling system: you carry exactly one physical object, money is itself an object, and every construction project, shopping trip and factory rebuild becomes manual labour. English reviews that discuss the no-inventory design skew 42% positive, far below the corpus average, and late-game builders hit it hardest. A representative 303-upvote review turns the tedium into a short story about carrying foundations across a dwarven ruin; others put a number on it: one reviewer estimates half of their first 90 minutes was spent re-doing dropped items.

> "Imagine you're happy with your tier 2 automation set up. It was somewhat temporary but now you're ready to break into tier 3 so you want to straighten your base up. You've been mining down in a cool looking dwarven ruin but your stuff is just laying all over the cave floor and it looks like you're some kind of dwarven hobo. [...] You can buy about 5 foundations at a time because you have to physically put them on a purchase platform and more than that doesn't fit." — English, 17.7 h, negative, 303 upvotes
>
> "The core mechanics of the game are good, but the lack of an inventory system dooms players to wasting a non-trivial portion of their in-game time on tedious / pointless make-work exercises." — English, 9.0 h, negative, 67 upvotes
>
> "You can only hold one item at the time witch makes buying and cleaning up items a huge tedious chore." — English, 56.3 h, negative, 46 upvotes
>
> "Of the around 90 minutes spent in the game so far, at least 45 has been spent re-doing tasks because I missed a target." — English, 1.6 h, negative, 5 upvotes
>
> "This game loves to just waste your time. It's a tedious nightmare because you have 0 inventory so you can only hold 1 object at a time. [...] Why do I have to carry my coins and put them in a bucket to buy things at a vendor and then go to another vendor across town only to realize I left my coins at the previous vendor." — English, 9.3 h, negative, 53 upvotes
>
> "There is no inventory. 3-1. Money is an item too... 3-2. Then at least let us use two hands, but it's always only one item... 3-3. Even to buy one item you need a cart to bring the goods and the money... I guess there are no bags in this world." — Korean, 1.8 h, negative, 3 upvotes (translated)

### 2. Placement, cursor and onboarding: "a second cursor" and no tutorial
The game drops players into the world with almost no in-game explanation, and the interaction scheme is unusual: the item you drop lands to the bottom-right of the screen rather than at the crosshair, with only a small shadow to guide it (a laser-sight option exists but is buried in settings). Reviews describe a 15-step panning routine taking an hour by the time every dropped scoop is redone, and a mandatory reliance on YouTube, the wiki and Discord. "Cursor / drop placement" is the second-most-negative theme measured (19% positive).

> "On first starting up the game I was just dropped in the world with no explanation of what I'm supposed to do, what the controls are, or how to interact with anything. [...] One of the clunkiest games I've played (outside of intentionally clunky games like surgeon simulator) where you have to manually pick up each and every item in your hands, set it down so you can pick up the next one, and repeat again and again and again." — English, 0.2 h, negative, 54 upvotes
>
> "the UI and controls are so janky and clunky and awkward that it actually made the game wholly unpleasant. I quit after about half an hour because I just couldn't stand the awkward cursor selections." — English, 0.4 h, negative, 113 upvotes
>
> "You can't look directly at the pan, you need to aim away and try to judge where the dirt is going to drop in the bottom right of your screen. Its not just dirt, but the ore you get from the dirt, the pan again, the bars from the smelter, the gold you get, etc." — English, 1.8 h, negative, 99 upvotes
>
> "The tutorial is not clear enough, it doesn't really explain much at all, I had to learn from guides and youtube videos. [...] Want to check your map? Go get your map. Want to switch tools? Climb above ground and grab one at a time." — English, 5.9 h, negative, 40 upvotes
>
> "you place below your right hand. Hidden the settings is a switch to turn on a laser sight so you can see where you are dropping it, but even with this, Minecraft has better "drop held item" than this game." — English, 0.4 h, negative, 5 upvotes

### 3. Physics and item loss: "yeeted into oblivion"
Objects in Hydroneer are simulated, and reviewers report nuggets exploding out of containers, ore rolling off conveyors, tools and equipment vanishing under the terrain, vehicles flipping into the sky, and items occasionally falling through the world entirely. Losing stock you spent hours mining — or a tool you cannot afford to replace — is a recurring rage point, and one of the oldest complaints in the corpus.

> "sometimes items get just yeeted into oblivion or dont enter into the container -dumping stuff from one container into another shouldn't depend on each single items drop trajectory but be a fixed motion where items can't miss (20 irons rolling awaaaaaay....)" — English, 1.0 h, negative, 238 upvotes
>
> "only to have half of my equipment disappear. The dev suggest buying foundations to put things on to help prevent things from disappearing." — English, 4.4 h, negative, 159 upvotes
>
> "physics have a tendency to freak out (ore/gem explosions, flipping truck = rocket ship)" — English, 11.9 h, negative, 87 upvotes
>

### 4. Save corruption and lost worlds
Save corruption is the most serious technical issue in the corpus: reviews reference it from 2022 onward, with the loudest wave in 2024–2025, describing entire saves — including the game's own backup save — being wiped or corrupted, sometimes after "save and quit" and sometimes mid-session. A 310-upvote review from February 2025, a 127-hour review from December 2025 and a May 2026 complaint all describe the same failure mode. The developer shipped a save-corruption fix in patch 3.0.9 (March 2025), but individual losses were still being reported through late 2025 and 2026.

> "49 hours into the game and loving it to come back to find my world is completely wiped [...] the discord literally has a section for shared save files to get you restarted somewhat after your save is corrupt and unrecoverable" — English, 49.8 h, negative, 310 upvotes
>
> "Every single save game (With ALL of the save options) have been corupted, lost 88 hours of work to a bug they said would fix YEARS ago DO NOT DOWNLOAD" — English, 88.3 h, negative, 2 upvotes
>
> "make sure you manually back up your saves regularly because simply saving and exiting the game can corrupt your saves (yes, that includes the "backup" save that isn't a backup - it'll get corrupted right along with your main save" — English, 16.4 h, negative, 18 upvotes
>
> "this game is +6 years old now and still has a bug that wipes all your progress when you dare to hit "save and quit"" — English, 1.4 h, negative, 9 upvotes

### 5. Performance collapse in the late game
Because every ore, gem and item is a simulated object with physics, big factories and large stockpiles melt the frame rate. Reviewers on high-end hardware (RTX 4070 Ti Super, RTX 3070, 32 GB RAM) report drops from 144 FPS to 20, minutes-long freezes when a bucket of gems is picked up, and saves where the frame rate in the endgame is permanently poor. ### 6. Multiplayer: no online co-op, a "Co-op" tag, and a years-long promise
Hydroneer spent 2020–2021 building expectation for true online multiplayer, then pivoted to split-screen via Steam Remote Play in 2.0. Because the store tags say "Co-op", buyers keep arriving expecting to play online with friends, and many bounce when they discover one player needs a controller, the two players share one screen, and Remote Play lags. It remains one of the most reliable sources of negative reviews — prominent again in September 2024 and May 2026.

### 7. Progression runs out: money becomes meaningless, Tier 2/3 don't matter
The game's economy is widely described as inverted: the first automated drill makes money faster than the game can absorb it, so plot purchases, vehicles and higher tiers stop feeling necessary. Reviewers report finishing the meaningful progression before their first big build is done, then looking at the remaining content — a few ores, four gems and a handful of craftable items — and asking "what now?". Even enthusiastic long-playtime reviews say the same thing.

### 8. Quests, tokens and farming grinds
The 2.1/2.2/3.0 updates gated higher tiers behind NPC quests paid in new, non-exchangeable currencies ("tokens"). Reviewers describe hundreds of low-value delivery runs, random requested items, and a farming economy where a single sprinkler costs 2,000+ tokens and a soup order can demand 600 crops — work that cannot be automated. The result, in a widely quoted summary, is an automation game that becomes a quest grind.

> "the game is frustratingly designed with multiple non-exchangeable currencies to gate progress with grinding delivery tasks. [...] you will have to do hundreds of them, each requiring driving to a person, finding what they want, driving back to your base, make it, drive back to the person to give them it then repeat." — English, 117.9 h, negative, 109 upvotes
>
> "This changed the game from small but good automation game to quest grind fest. And I don't think so players wanted that." — English, 59.9 h, negative, 57 upvotes
>

### 9. "Finished": the paid DLC, the abandonment feeling and bait updates

### 10. Journey to Volcalidus: a bigger map with the same problems

## The ending and secrets (SPOILERS)

Hydroneer has no campaign, no cutscenes and no credits roll. The closest thing to an ending is the **King's quest**, the base game's long NPC questline: to reach the King you must first satisfy four knightly trials — jewelry/metallurgy work, farm-token crops, and finally a specific **fish (a 1.5-weight lobster)**, which reviewers across years describe as the single worst gate in the game. A 98.9-hour reviewer unable to land the lobster (with a level-8 rod, the best in the game) posted the definitive complaint; others describe the same quest as mandatory boredom. Reaching the King and receiving his reward is the base game's finale for completionists.

> "if you want to see the king, there are 4 knights before you get to see the little s**t sitting on his throne, sh**ing his pants. Two knight needs metallurgy/jewelry to get past him. ## What reviewers say about value and who should buy it

**Recommended for:**
- Factory/automation tinkerers who enjoy conveyor logic, pipe networks and optimizing layouts ("Satisfactory/Factorio-adjacent, but tactile").
- Players who like physical, low-HUD sandbox games and are willing to learn controls through experimentation, the wiki and Discord.
- Chill, podcast-friendly gamers — there is no combat, no hunger and no fail state.
- Patient players who can sink 50–200 hours and set their own goals; value per dollar is repeatedly called excellent, especially at sale prices (~$5–8).
- Parents, couples and friends who want a couch/Remote Play split-screen experience and accept its limits.
- Streamer-culture fans who enjoy chaos, meme runs and deliberately breaking the simulation.

**Not recommended for:**
- Players who need a clear tutorial, quest markers, a map and strong early direction — the first hour is the game's weakest and the refund window is not enough to see the good part.
- Anyone who wants real online co-op with friends; the "Co-op" tag means split-screen/Remote Play only.
- Players who dislike inventory-less handling and manual logistics; the tedium scales with your ambitions.
- Those with low tolerance for jank, physics bugs and the risk of losing a save; manual save backups are advised by reviewers.
- People who need a defined ending or endgame; money outpaces the things to spend it on, and the King's quest is the only finale.
- Buyers expecting the DLC to fix the base game's pacing; most recommend playing the base game first, on sale.

## Caveats

- The corpus is every review retrievable through Steam's public review API on 15 September 2026: 28,474 of the 28,477 counted by Steam. Three negative reviews were no longer retrievable; Steam totals and upvote counts change over time.
- Steam's `filter=all` ordering is unstable for this app — its cursor cycles and returns duplicated batches. The full corpus was therefore pulled with `filter=recent`, which paginates the same complete review set without duplication. Review texts and timestamps are as returned by the API; reviews edited after posting appear with their original creation date and current text.
- Quotes are verbatim apart from light cleanup of line breaks, markup tags and Steam's censored-profanity characters; elisions are marked `[...]`. Non-English quotes were translated and marked "(translated)". Attribution gives language, playtime at review, sentiment and upvotes as of the scrape date.
- Keyword theme counts are approximate and English-biased: the same review can mention several themes, and much of the most detailed praise and criticism was written in German, Russian, Chinese, French, Polish, Czech, Japanese and Korean, where keyword matching catches fewer mentions.
- The corpus includes jokes, memes and low-effort reviews (247 reviews are empty), and long-tail praise is dominated by high-playtime players while negatives are over-represented among players under 2 hours — both ends of the playtime distribution should be read with that in mind.
