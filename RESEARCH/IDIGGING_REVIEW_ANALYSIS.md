## TL;DR

iDigging is a low-budget, first-person co-op digging and home-renovation game with a comedic suburban stealth layer: after your wife leaves you for your wealthier next-door neighbor, you begin digging an illegal shaft in your suburban backyard to strike it rich, buy furniture, and furnish your empty home. You must dodge your peeping neighbor (throwing tomatoes or hitting him with a shovel), hide the shaft under single-use tarps or fake turf, and evade arriving police inspectors, all while digging past underground caverns toward the center of the earth.

Reviewers describe a deeply polarized experience characterized by hilarious chaos in short bursts, undermined by severe technical friction:
- **The core escalation loop and budget humor work:** For $0.60 to $3, digging greedily, buying drills and explosives, throwing tomatoes at the neighbor, buying an alarm dog, and furnishing the house offers genuine chuckles and addictive progression for 2–3 hours.
- **The co-op networking is catastrophically broken:** The game generates and updates terrain voxel chunks exclusively around the host. As soon as client players venture roughly 300 meters away, the subterranean world de-renders beneath them, plunging them into an endless void fall.
- **The "China" ending triggers accidentally:** Falling through collision holes or clipping through floor meshes drops players straight into the void trigger for the game's final cutscene (digging to China), unintentionally completing the entire game in 10–20 minutes without touching progression.
- **The stealth mechanics wear thin over time:** The recurring police timer (roughly 6.5–10 minutes, faster if the neighbor is ignored) and persistent neighbor harassment force players to halt excavation and repeatedly teleport to the surface to pay fines or swat the neighbor, turning mid-game digging into an annoying logistical hassle.
- **Unwelcome political elements:** A collectible Trump coin labeled "#trump" with the slogan "fight fight fight" sparked a wave of negative reviews in the March 2025 launch window over real-world politics injected into an escapist digging simulator. The developer later removed the political branding, leaving "just a dig coin."
- **No pause and little comfort:** Reviewers report that the pause screen does not stop the police timer, the alert phone cannot be muted, and digging cannot be rebound off the held mouse button—one player developed arm cramps from it.

## What reviewers criticize (the bad)

### 1. Multiplayer desync and host-centric chunk unloading
The foremost technical disaster in the corpus stems from network architecture: voxel chunks and terrain collider data are calculated strictly relative to the host's position. When guest clients venture further than ~200–300 meters down a shaft while the host remains near the surface, the ground unloads entirely beneath the client's feet, causing them to jitter violently and fall through the world. Joining and hosting fail outright for some players, lobbies must be restarted repeatedly, and several groups report all of their PCs running at their limits. TWEN's standing reply attributes this to the host's machine—"lags and teleportation depend on the internet speed of the lobby owner and the power of his computer"—and recommends that the strongest PC host, an explanation the detailed analysis in the corpus disputes.

> "the big game breaking issue in multiplayer that people are complaining about is caused by the world only updating relative to the host's location, it has nothing to do with weak hardware or an unstable connection. ### 6. Save files, disappearing items, and unreliable achievements
The save system exhibits major defects. Upon saving, quitting, and reloading, players often find that custom-excavated complex shafts have been wiped and replaced with a generic vertical hole; one reviewer reports a hole down to only 50% of their previous depth. Placed furniture, lamps, and other decorative items above ground level routinely vanish, resetting progress toward the house-renovation ending; one reviewer watched their carefully organized, stacked items disappear. Other reload glitches include grass growing back over already-dug holes with resources left floating in place, and a purchased alarm dog vanishing after every re-login. There is only one save slot and it is shared between solo and multiplayer, so loading multiplayer can overwrite a solo world (and vice versa). ### 8. Shallow, unfair enemy design
Outside of the opening tutorial section, enemy variety collapses to roughly three archetypes: blue bugs that spawn abruptly beneath (or above) players and deal unavoidable damage—several reviewers call them a "HP tax"—crocodiles in toxic water and a pig guarding loot rooms, with the pig able to one-shot players. Most enemies cannot be killed with tools or guns at all; the pig is only killable with bombs, and the crocodile is effectively unkillable. Loot-guarding animals instead teleport players back to the yard and take their money. In multiplayer, enemies are not synchronized between screens, and the vegetable-firing weapons are decorative: reviewers report they do nothing to enemies or the neighbor.

### 11. Accessibility and comfort gaps
The corpus contains no colorblind, FOV, subtitle, or motion-sickness requests, but it does surface a consistent set of comfort problems that limit who can play comfortably.

- **No functioning pause.** Pressing ESC does not stop the game's timers: one player returned from a phone call to find the police had already punished them, and another lost all their money while the menu said paused.
- **No way to mute the alert phone.** The dog's alarm rings through the in-game phone, and one reviewer begs for a mute function.
- **No key rebinding.** Digging requires holding the left mouse button; a reviewer developed arm cramps and asks to bind digging to a keyboard key. The options menu is minimal—it does offer Y/X camera inversion and V-Sync, added after players asked—but a reviewer notes the configuration "could stop pretending it doesn't exist."
- **Readability and clarity.** Tutorial text vanishes after about 10 seconds, the small attachable lamps barely light anything, and unmineable walls share the same texture as diggable dirt, so players cannot tell where they may dig.
- **Motion discomfort.** Constant teleportation and rubber-banding made at least one reviewer dizzy.

> "even though it says paused. It's not. So if you get a call you will come back to find you've lost all your money." — English, 3.5 h, negative, 2 upvotes

### 12. Performance and crashes on capable hardware
Performance complaints are not limited to weak machines. Reviewers report 30 FPS on an RTX 3080 Ti, stutter deep underground on an RTX 4060 Ti, no difference between low and ultra on a system above the recommended spec, PCs heating up, an RTX 4070 blue-screen, and a full PC crash during the tutorial that left the player unable to move their camera. Hosts can also crash when a guest spams shop purchases. The developer asks players for their specs and recommends enabling V-Sync to cap GPU usage, and says the cause of the heavy CPU/GPU load has been identified with optimizations rolling out.

## The ending and secrets (SPOILERS)

iDigging features two separate narrative endings, along with notable subterranean easter eggs and widely exploited engine bugs:

### 1. Ending 1: The "China" Core Breakthrough
Digging vertically down past approximately 1,000 meters through increasingly dense stone strata culminates in breaking through the earth's crust and tumbling straight out of the sky onto the other side of the world: **China**. A comedic cinematic sequence plays showing the protagonist emerging in a stereotypical Chinese pavilion landscape before credits roll.

> "China ending- It was a very fun and cool game thanks for making it." — German, 1.0 h, positive, 2 upvotes

### 2. Ending 2: The House Renovation / Ex-Wife Reconciliation
The secondary victory condition ignores digging depth and focuses purely on domestic prosperity. By hauling enough valuable furniture, ornate lamps, luxury beds, sofas, TVs, and underground gems back to your home, you max out the property's value meter. Upon reaching 100% home restoration, a cutscene triggers where your ex-wife leaves the wealthy neighbor and returns to your arms.

> "I was greeted with two completely stupid Endings, showing that the Game doesn't take itself seriously." — English, 4.1 h, negative

### 3. Exploits, Secrets, and Mechanical Oddities
- **The Crouch X-Ray Exploit:** Crouching inside narrow, newly dug 1x1 shafts forces the player camera into the surrounding voxel terrain mesh, culling adjacent geometry and granting full underground X-ray vision. This allows players to pinpoint all high-value mineral veins, chests, and hidden chambers through solid rock without blind digging.

> "I beat the game with both endings in 3 hours using a bug (crouched under a texture) — found it accidentally right when I started playing. You just need to dig a small pit, crouch, and if the character gets cramped — you can see all resources through textures." — Russian, 3.8 h, positive, 3 upvotes (translated)

- **Infinite Chest Loot Respawn:** Pre-placed underground loot rooms and secret caches do not preserve their empty state across sessions. Saving and immediately reloading repopulates all high-value items, golden cats, and lightbulbs, completely bypassing the need to mine regular ore.

> "all secret caches restore their contents after we save and reload again." — Russian, 15.2 h, positive, 11 upvotes (translated)

- **The Underground Vegetable AR Rifle:** Hidden within deep subterranean chambers is an assault rifle that fires vegetables (primarily tomatoes). Unlike thrown vegetables, however, gunfire appears to be purely decorative: reviewers report that the guns do nothing to enemies or the neighbor, with one calling all weapons "just props."
- **Post-Game Police Achievement Lockout:** Once either ending is reached, police visits permanently stop. As a result, players hunting the Steam achievement *"Survive 20 police visits"* find themselves permanently locked out and are forced to launch a fresh save and sit idle for 2+ hours just waiting for police door-knocks. The achievement also does not retain progress across sittings: one reviewer notes it "doesn't pop if you leave the game" and must be earned in one save file in a single session.

> "when beating the game the police no longer comes so that means i would have to start a new save to get the achivement "survive 20 police visits" and apparently this will take me at least over 2 hours of me just sitting being afk." — English, 2.7 h, positive, 1 upvotes

## Player-suggested improvements (mechanics, QoL, content)

Ideas that players explicitly ask for, kept as design input:

- **Input and comfort:** bind digging to a keyboard key; add a real options/config menu; make the pause work; add a mute for the alert phone.
- **Storage and selling:** a bigger or expandable warehouse; an auto-sell system; a "Sell All" button and category sorting for items.
- **Traversal:** a buyable jetpack or an elevator; a sprint instead of the dash (and a jump); a placeable teleport at the last dig site to avoid re-entering the shaft.
- **Furniture and house:** vertical rotation and wall-mounting; shelves and wall racks for weapons; more furniture and decor; wallpaper and floor customization; character skins. (Placed items can already be locked in place with the B key—the developer points this out in a reply—but reviewers repeatedly miss it, so the tutorial should teach it.)
- **Content:** more caves, catacombs, challenges and creatures so the underground is worth exploring; a second game phase after the China ending (one reviewer suggests digging on to Japan); a Switch port.

- **Stealth balance (both directions):** some players want the neighbor/police loop to matter more (a French reviewer notes you can leave the hole uncovered and the only sanction is a modest fine), while others want the dog to reliably repel the neighbor.

## What reviewers say about value and who should buy it

### Recommended for:
- **Budget co-op groups:** Friend circles looking for an evening of chaotic, slapstick fun for under $3 where game-breaking physics glitches are embraced as part of the comedy.
- **Casual digging enthusiasts in "No Enemies" mode:** Players who want a casual, low-stakes excavation loop to enjoy while listening to podcasts or watching videos, provided they toggle enemies off at the start (accepting that spiders may still spawn in some builds).
- **Fans of quirky base-building and scavenger progression:** Players who enjoy furnishing an empty house with weird trinkets, coffins, televisions, and shiny minerals unearthed from subterranean pits.
- **Trophy hunters and achievement completionists:** A swift 100% completion list (roughly 1.5 to 5 hours), provided police visits are farmed before completing the main storyline and the achievements trigger correctly.

### Not recommended for:
- **Serious co-op survival players:** Non-host clients will quickly encounter severe world-unloading bugs and void falls that make systematic excavation impossible.
- **Purists seeking smooth tactile excavation:** Anyone expecting the refined voxel physics, responsive digging audio, and atmospheric immersion of *A Game About Digging a Hole*.
- **Players frustrated by repetitive chore interruptions:** Anyone who dislikes having deep-mining concentration broken every few minutes by stealth alarms, police door-knocks, or tiny inventory restrictions.
- **Players with RSI or input needs:** Digging is locked to a held mouse button with no rebinding, and there is no functional pause or alert mute.
- **Those averse to real-world politics in gaming:** Players who are put off by political memes or collectibles referencing modern political figures in an escapist indie title (the Trump branding was later removed).

## Caveats

- **Corpus coverage:** This document analyzes every review retrievable via Steam's public API on 14 September 2026 (862 of 862 reviews counted live by Steam, capturing 100% of the live review corpus).
- **Language weighting:** While English represents 31.3% of reviews (270 records), Russian (30.4%, 262 records) and German (20.3%, 175 records) together constitute over half the total dataset. Quotes from non-English reviews have been translated verbatim to provide balanced representation across all player regions.
- **Patch and early access status:** Reviews began on 5 March 2025 (early access) and the game reached 1.0 two months later, followed by multiple patches. The corpus contains both praise for early fixes and reports that 1.0 and later updates regressed collision, terrain persistence and inventory size; the Trump branding on the buried coin was removed after launch-week complaints. Fundamental host-centric chunk desync, collision snags and save flaws persist in late-2026 reviews.
- **Quote reliability:** The corpus contains obvious joke reviews and a few high-effort essays that read as AI-generated (one 12 h review credits the game with oxygen management and structural stability, which no other review mentions). Quotes were chosen from reviews whose claims are corroborated elsewhere wherever possible.
- **Subjective nature of reviews:** Steam reviews reflect subjective player impressions at the time of play rather than exhaustive technical audits; playtimes cited represent hours logged at the exact moment each review was posted.
