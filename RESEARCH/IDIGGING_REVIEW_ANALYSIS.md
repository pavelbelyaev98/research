## TL;DR

iDigging is a low-budget, first-person co-op digging and home-renovation game with a comedic suburban stealth layer: after your wife leaves you for your wealthier next-door neighbor, you begin digging an illegal shaft in your suburban backyard to strike it rich, buy furniture, and furnish your empty home. You must dodge your peeping neighbor (throwing tomatoes or hitting him with a shovel), evade or bribe arriving police inspectors, and dig past underground caverns down toward the center of the earth.

Reviewers describe a deeply polarized experience characterized by hilarious chaos in short bursts, undermined by severe technical friction:
- **The core escalation loop and budget humor work:** For $0.60 to $3, digging greedily, buying drills and explosives, throwing tomatoes at the neighbor, buying an alarm dog, and furnishing the house offers genuine chuckles and addictive progression for 2–3 hours.
- **The co-op networking is catastrophically broken:** The game generates and updates terrain voxel chunks exclusively around the host. As soon as client players venture roughly 300 meters away, the subterranean world de-renders beneath them, plunging them into an endless void fall.
- **The "China" ending triggers accidentally:** Falling through collision holes or clipping through floor meshes drops players straight into the void trigger for the game's final cutscene (digging to China), unintentionally completing the entire game in 10–20 minutes without touching progression.
- **The stealth mechanics wear thin over time:** The recurring 10-minute police timer and persistent neighbor harassment force players to halt excavation and repeatedly teleport to the surface to pay fines or swat the neighbor, turning mid-game digging into an annoying logistical hassle.
- **Unwelcome political elements:** The discovery of a collectible Trump coin labeled "#trump" with the slogan "fight fight fight" sparked a dedicated wave of negative reviews over real-world politics injected into an escapist digging simulator.

## What reviewers praise (the good)

### 1. The satisfying escalation of the core excavation loop
When working as intended, the fundamental loop of digging downward, unearthing ores and buried relics, hauling loot to the surface, and investing in upgraded shovels, heavy-duty drills, and high-yield explosives delivers the quintessential incremental rush that fans of the digging sub-genre crave.

### 2. Comedic multiplayer chaos and co-op shenanigans
The option to dig holes with up to four friends (5-player co-op) is widely highlighted as the game's strongest selling point. Even when physics glitch out, players report laughing through the absurdity together, splitting up roles between deep-shaft excavators and surface-level shopkeepers.

### 3. The comedic stealth layer: neighbor, tomatoes, dog, and police
Unlike purely meditative digging titles, iDigging overlays a narrative of suburban spite and illegality. Digging in your yard is unlawful, prompting your nosy neighbor to peer over the fence. Players praise the absurd counter-measures: chucking tomatoes at the neighbor's face, clobbering him with a shovel, purchasing camouflage turf, or buying an alarm dog to guard the yard.

## What reviewers criticize (the bad)

### 1. Multiplayer desync and host-centric chunk unloading
The foremost technical disaster in the corpus stems from network architecture: voxel chunks and terrain collider data are calculated strictly relative to the host's position. When guest clients venture further than ~200–300 meters down a shaft while the host remains near the surface, the ground unloads entirely beneath the client's feet, causing them to jitter violently and fall through the world.

> "the big game breaking issue in multiplayer that people are complaining about is caused by the world only updating relative to the host's location, it has nothing to do with weak hardware or an unstable connection. When a client is around 300 meters from the host, the world starts unloading for them, causing them to jitter out and fall through the world" — English, 1.4 h, negative, 54 upvotes
>
> "If you're not the host, the game literally gets unplayable at some point." — English, 2.9 h, negative, 4 upvotes
>
> "Unable to play multiplayer! It is always lagging and teleporting us every where!" — English, 1.4 h, negative, 1 upvotes

The neighbor peeps over the fence on a relentless timer, forcing players to stop whatever they are doing, hit the surface teleport button, chuck a tomato, and drop all the way back down. When the police arrive every 10 minutes, players must sit through prolonged door-knocking animations and inspection routines or suffer an inescapable $500 fine and beatdown animation.

### 5. Severe inventory and storage capacity bottlenecks
Backpack inventory limits are excruciatingly tight from the outset, and storage upgrades scale steeply in cost. In multiplayer, storage fills almost instantaneously: as soon as one player returns to the depths after emptying their pockets, teammates have already filled the collection bins, leading to severe logistical gridlock.

### 6. Save file flaws: reset holes and disappearing furniture
The save system exhibits major defects. Upon saving, quitting, and reloading, players often find that custom-excavated complex shafts have been wiped and replaced with generic vertical holes. Worse, placed furniture and decorative items above ground level routinely vanish upon session reloads, resetting progress toward the house-renovation ending.

### 7. Floating dirt particles, snagging, and traversal friction
Voxel destruction frequently leaves behind invisible or microscopic dirt fragments suspended in mid-air. Players falling down their main shafts repeatedly snag against these imperceptible collision points, halting momentum and requiring awkward mid-air tool swinging to clear passage.

Outside of the opening tutorial section, enemy variety collapses. Players encounter blue bugs that spawn abruptly beneath their feet and deal damage without visual warning, stationary crocodiles in green acid/toxic pools, and a single pig. Because tool combat is rudimentary and weapons are scarce, fighting subterranean mobs feels clunky rather than engaging.

## The ending and secrets (SPOILERS)

iDigging features two separate narrative endings, along with notable subterranean easter eggs and widely exploited engine bugs:

### 1. Ending 1: The "China" Core Breakthrough
Digging vertically down past approximately 1,000 meters through increasingly dense stone strata culminates in breaking through the earth's crust and tumbling straight out of the sky onto the other side of the world: **China**. A comedic cinematic sequence plays showing the protagonist emerging in a stereotypical Chinese pavilion landscape before credits roll.

> "China ending- It was a very fun and cool game thanks for making it." — German, 1.0 h, positive, 2 upvotes

### 2. Ending 2: The House Renovation / Ex-Wife Reconciliation
The secondary victory condition ignores digging depth and focuses purely on domestic prosperity. By hauling enough valuable furniture, ornate lamps, luxury beds, sofas, TVs, and underground gems back to your home, you max out the property's value meter. Upon reaching 100% home restoration, a cutscene triggers where your ex-wife leaves the wealthy neighbor and returns to your arms.

> "I was greeted with two completely stupid Endings, showing that the Game doesn't take itself seriously." — English, 4.1 h, negative

### 3. Exploits, Secrets, and Mechanical Oddities
- **The Crouch X-Ray Exploit:** Crouching inside narrow, newly dug 1x1 shafts forces the player camera into the surrounding voxel terrain mesh, culling adjacent geometry and granting full underground X-ray vision. This allows players to pinpoint all high-value mineral veins, chests, and hidden chambers through solid rock without blind digging.

> "I beat the game with both endings in 3 hours using a bug (crouched under a texture) — found it accidentally right when I started playing. You just need to dig a small pit, crouch, and if the character gets cramped — you can see all resources through textures." — Russian, 3.8 h, positive, 3 upvotes (translated)

- **Infinite Chest Loot Respawn:** Pre-placed underground loot rooms and secret caches do not preserve their empty state across sessions. Saving and immediately reloading repopulates all high-value items, golden cats, and lightbulbs, completely bypassing the need to mine regular ore.

> "all secret caches restore their contents after we save and reload again." — Russian, 15.2 h, positive, 11 upvotes (translated)

- **The Underground Vegetable AR Rifle:** Hidden within deep subterranean chambers is an assault rifle that shoots vegetables (primarily tomatoes), allowing players to stockpile long-range ammunition to swat the neighbor from across the yard.
- **Post-Game Police Achievement Lockout:** Once either ending is reached, police visits permanently stop. As a result, players hunting the Steam achievement *"Survive 20 police visits"* find themselves permanently locked out and are forced to launch a fresh save and sit idle for 2+ hours just waiting for police door-knocks.

> "when beating the game the police no longer comes so that means i would have to start a new save to get the achivement "survive 20 police visits" and apparently this will take me at least over 2 hours of me just sitting being afk." — English, 2.7 h, positive, 1 upvotes

## What reviewers say about value and who should buy it

### Recommended for:
- **Budget co-op groups:** Friend circles looking for an evening of chaotic, slapstick fun for under $3 where game-breaking physics glitches are embraced as part of the comedy.
- **Casual digging enthusiasts in "Chill Mode":** Players who want a casual, low-stakes excavation loop to enjoy while listening to podcasts or watching videos, provided they toggle enemies off at the start.
- **Fans of quirky base-building and scavenger progression:** Players who enjoy furnishing an empty house with weird trinkets, coffins, televisions, and shiny minerals unearthed from subterranean pits.
- **Trophy hunters and achievement completionists:** A relatively swift 100% completion list achievable within 3 to 5 hours (provided police visits are farmed before completing the main storyline).

### Not recommended for:
- **Serious co-op survival players:** Non-host clients will quickly encounter severe world-unloading bugs and void falls that make systematic excavation impossible.
- **Purists seeking smooth tactile excavation:** Anyone expecting the refined voxel physics, responsive digging audio, and atmospheric immersion of *A Game About Digging a Hole*, *Dome Keeper*, or *Motherload*.
- **Players frustrated by repetitive chore interruptions:** Anyone who dislikes having deep-mining concentration broken every few minutes by stealth alarms, police door-knocks, or tiny inventory restrictions.
- **Those averse to real-world politics in gaming:** Players who are put off by political memes or collectibles referencing modern political figures in an escapist indie title.

## Caveats

- **Corpus coverage:** This document analyzes every review retrievable via Steam's public API on 14 September 2026 (862 of 862 reviews counted live by Steam, capturing 100% of the live review corpus).
- **Language weighting:** While English represents 31.3% of reviews (270 records), Russian (30.4%, 262 records) and German (20.3%, 175 records) together constitute over half the total dataset. Quotes from non-English reviews have been translated verbatim to provide balanced representation across all player regions.
- **Patch and early access status:** iDigging received multiple patches following its March/May 2025 release window. Certain severe early bugs—such as instant item wipes and initial multiplayer crashes—were actively addressed by the developer, though fundamental host-centric chunk desync and collision snags persist in the final corpus.
- **Subjective nature of reviews:** Steam reviews reflect subjective player impressions at the time of play rather than exhaustive technical audits; playtimes cited represent hours logged at the exact moment each review was posted.
