## TL;DR

One Man's Trash is a short, cheap, satisfying "dig-hole" game that is easy to recommend for a relaxed weekend — and just as easy to describe as a 3–5 hour clone of *A Game About Digging a Hole* that overstays its welcome and hides a horror game inside a cozy one.

**Players overwhelmingly agree on:**
- The core loop (vacuum → sell → upgrade → go deeper) is genuinely satisfying, addictive and relaxing.
- The vacuum + dirt-spitting (platform building) mechanic is a real improvement on the shovel/drill formula.
- The price matches the content; it's a "cheaper than a beer" impulse buy.
- Collectibles, pop-culture references and changing underground locations keep the first hours fresh.

**Players overwhelmingly agree it should have done differently:**
- The **worms / horror elements** are the single most controversial and most disliked feature — unadvertised, inescapable, and frequent. The later "cozy mode" update exists because of this.
- The game is **too short and too shallow** for its price by many accounts; upgrades are mostly small percentage boosts and the toolset never evolves.
- **Bugs, crashes, no autosave, lost progress and broken achievements** — technical issues are a recurring complaint.
- The **rope-only traversal** (no jetpack), health/sandwich RNG and inventory limits make the back half tedious.

## What reviewers praise (the good)

### 1. A genuinely satisfying, hypnotic core loop
The most consistent praise. Watching dirt vanish into the vacuum is "oddly satisfying," and the dig → sell → upgrade → dig loop earns frequent comparisons to PowerWash Simulator and "mining in Minecraft." Many reviewers describe it as a zen/cozy game to play with a podcast on.

> "one of those oddly satisfying games. It's a great zone out game" — English, 4 h, positive
>
> "No thoughts, Head empty, I dig hole :D" — English, 10.4 h, positive
>
> "This is one of my favorite mind-numbing games. after a long and stressful day at work i like to come home and play this to unwind." — English, 4.8 h, positive
>
> "It's perfect to pass time without needing to think… great to play while listening to a podcast." — Brazilian Portuguese, 6.8 h, positive (translated)

### 2. "Just one more run" addictiveness
Even mixed and negative reviews often admit the loop hooks them. Several reviewers describe losing track of time and finishing the game in one sitting.

> "the urge to keep playing is ridiculously strong. Even when I thought, ugh, I'm getting bored of this, I had to keep going, 'just 10 more minutes.' Those 10 minutes turned a one-hour session into three hours." — English, 7 h, positive
>
> "every time I told myself I was going to stop… I would do one more run going down the rope… and just like that, it's almost 6am" — English, 3.8 h, positive
>
> "This game is more addictive than drugs, alcohol, coffee, cigarettes…" — English, 2.9 h, positive

### 3. The vacuum instead of a shovel/drill is a real improvement
Reviewers repeatedly highlight two quality-of-life wins over *A Game About Digging a Hole*: no leftover dirt specks/pebbles, and the ability to "spit" dirt back out to rebuild terrain and staircases.

> "I like using a vacuum instead of a drill, makes it much easier to not leave specks of dirt everywhere that can be extremely annoying in others of this genre." — English, 6.8 h, positive
>
> "You dont get those tiny pebbles left flying in the air that can kill you as you ascent like other dig games." — English, 3.8 h, positive
>
> "You're also able to build using the dirt you suck up, letting you shape your environment a little more neatly and form staircases back up." — English, 3.6 h, positive

### 4. Collectibles, references and discoveries down deep
Hundreds of junk items, rare collectibles with joke descriptions, pop-culture references (reviewers mention a Pulp Fiction nod and "a certain infamous game cartridge"), cars, train cars, mines and a huge spaceship. Many call hunting them the best part.

> "A TON of rare unique items, that are references to movies and videogames so you always have the joy of understanding that reference" — English, 6 h, positive
>
> "the deeper you go, the weirder it gets" — English, 7 h, positive
>
> "I actually had a big silly grin on my face when I found a certain infamous game cartridge!" — English, 11.1 h, positive

### 5. Presentation, lighting and atmosphere
The cartoon look, colored placeable lamps and the darkening depths are commonly called pretty, and the underground mood works for the genre.

> "cool chill game. the graphics is very pleasant." — English, 11.2 h, positive
>
> "Grapics are pretty, lighting and changing environments keep it interesting." — English, 7.4 h, positive
>
> "I adore the game's lighting that you can place and that looks like garlands offering a multitude of colors." — French, 8.7 h, positive (translated)

### 6. Fair price for what it is
The budget price is the most common counter-argument to every length complaint. Reviewers repeatedly call it a bargain, a "cheaper than a beer" purchase, or say they got exactly their money's worth.

> "The price is an absolute steal. I have 16 hours in already and there's still more to do." — English, 16.4 h, positive
>
> "Nice chill game. completed in 2.5 hours. for £4.50. cheaper than a beer." — English, 2.5 h, positive
>
> "Great game, thanks to developer for not over pricing it." — English, 20.3 h, positive

### 7. Content expansions and options added after launch
Reviewers note new difficulty modes, Abyss mode and especially the **cozy mode that removes the worms** — the fix most asked for in early negative reviews. Reviewers also report the game runs fine on Steam Deck after remapping controls.

> "There are new game modes now, in the cozy mode you can play the game without the only annoying part of it: the worms" — Brazilian Portuguese, 10.1 h, positive (translated)
>
> "Nice that they added some difficulty modes." — English, 2.6 h, positive

## What reviewers criticize (the bad)

### 1. The worms / horror elements — the #1 complaint
The game presents itself as a cozy digging game, then introduces worms below a certain depth. Negative and positive reviews alike call them jumpscares, unadvertised, inescapable, too frequent and too damaging. Several reviewers say the worms alone ruined the game for them; others call it a "horror game" they didn't sign up for. (The cozy mode added later addresses this, but the complaint dominates reviews from before and after.)

> "I was unaware that there would be jumpscare worms, as the game is not labelled as a horror type game" — English, 6.1 h, negative
>
> "getting attacked by giant man-eating worms was not part of the advertised experience and killed the fun stone dead for me" — English, 2.8 h, negative
>
> "Please give an option to turn off monsters… worm coming after you every 5 seconds" — English, 1.8 h, negative
>
> "The worm follows you home… it can reappear 10 seconds later… there is no way to fight back at all" — Korean, 10.5 h, negative (translated)
>
> "Please label it as a horror game." — Simplified Chinese, 3.8 h, negative (translated)

Even positive reviewers frequently name the worms as their one complaint, and several wished for a weapon, trap or off-switch instead of just running away.

### 2. Too short / too little content
Reviews consistently report finishing in ~2.5–5 hours, and many feel that is not enough. A common accusation: the full game is barely longer than the demo.

> "Played almost 3 hours of the Demo… found it was only 10-20 minutes longer than the Demo and got to the end of it in about an hour with all the upgrades. Moral of the story: Just play the Demo." — English, 4.7 h, negative
>
> "there is only around 3 hours of gameplay… Not enough content for $5 imo. Wait til it's on sale for ~$1-3" — English, 3.8 h, negative
>
> "too quickly finished played… you'd need an 'open' underground world like Minecraft… more branches, more dangers like lava, more to collect" — German, 13.8 h, positive-with-criticism (translated)

### 3. Upgrades and progression feel shallow
The most common design criticism: the tool never changes, only its numbers, and the numbers aren't felt. Reviewers dislike the tier/license gating (e.g. a ~$20,000 membership wall) and pointless cosmetics.

> "nothing changes in the game. Nothing at all… There are upgrades that have invisible effects (+5% range, +5% power)… the gameplay or the tool you have don't evolve." — English, 2 h, negative
>
> "Upgrades are tiny and lack impact. You often don't feel the effect of anything you're buying" — English, 6.2 h, negative
>
> "I wish there were different tools you could use or actual upgraded vacuums but instead it is all just different coloured vacuums or boosting the stats of your vacuum." — English, 22.8 h, positive-with-criticism

Related complaints: only **two inventory slots for rare/temporary items** (picking a third deletes the first), discount coupons that expire before they can be used, and randomly colored lamps that encourage install/remove rerolling.

> "there's % off coupons for the shop upgrades but they're so far down and time out so quickly you can't even use them anyway so what's the point?" — English, 3.6 h, negative

### 4. Bugs, crashes and lost progress
Technical problems appear across languages: crashes shortly after the intro and at depth, stutters, falling through textures, falling FPS over time, camera drift, broken keybindings, and — repeatedly — **no autosave**.

> "Crashes right after the intro, before I can play." — English, 0.4 h, negative
>
> "10 hours in I ran into a bad bug where all the dirt re-appeared on loading back into the game. This left me stuck… got randomly killed by a giant snake without warning." — English, 29.2 h, negative
>
> "No autosave; if the game crashes after hours of play, the save is lost." — Simplified Chinese, 2.7 h, negative (translated)
>
> "No optimization: FPS drops over time from 144 to 30… constant falling through textures." — Russian, 6.4 h / 5.1 h, negative (translated)
>
> "No autosave in 2025 is kinda crazy though" — English, 8.3 h, positive-with-criticism

Achievements were also reported broken (the 1M money and 20-rare-item achievements), and late-game reward bugs were reported by at least one reviewer.

### 5. Traversal, rope and health friction
There is no jetpack; the only way up is a single central rope. As players widen the pit they must rebuild ramps, and missing the rope means falling damage or death. Health regenerates slowly and cannot be bought, so some reviewers waited AFK for a sandwich to spawn.

> "the system for getting back up — a single rope in the center, no jetpack or anything — is fine at first, but when you start clearing the place it becomes a pain to rebuild walkways to reach that damn rope." — French, 6.5 h, negative (translated)
>
> "Having absolutely no way of buying health… me going AFK for 5 minutes to let my health slowly refill is just a waste of time" — English, 6.1 h, negative

One reviewer also reported a potential softlock by digging out the top area so the rope can no longer be reached.

### 6. Derivative of "A Game About Digging a Hole"
A recurring criticism is that the game is a clone with more content but less balance — despite many positive reviewers actually preferring it to the original.

> "It's basically a carbon copy of the game about digging a hole… The game offers different skins for collecting them all but really it's a red [dirt]-covered vacuum or other color [dirt]-covered vacuum." — English, 6.3 h, negative
>
> "One Man's Trash is almost a good incremental game, but doesn't quite get there… doesn't understand the balance that game struck to keep it engaging." — English, 6.2 h, negative

### 7. Repetitive, especially in the back half
Even favorable reviews warn that "what you see in the first five minutes is all you're going to get" — a fixed, non-random map and one verb (suck) make later hours a slog for some.

> "relaxing for the first hour or two then just a painful, annoying slog" — English, 3.6 h, negative
>
> "The game is also too long given the lack of variety." — English, 6.2 h, negative

### 8. Miscellaneous
- **No FOV slider**, reported to cause motion sickness/nausea; a common request in positive and negative reviews.
- **Controller support**: missing or broken relative to the demo; Steam Deck works after manual remapping.
- **Sound design**: some call it lacking; one Russian reviewer found the music annoying and suspected AI generation, while other reviewers call the soundtrack relaxing.
- **Ending**: several say the payoff is abrupt or unsatisfying; one French reviewer reported both end-game rewards arriving bugged.

## Who the reviews say should buy it

**Recommended for:** fans of *A Game About Digging a Hole*, PowerWash-style "chore simulators", and anyone wanting a cheap, low-stress game to play with a podcast for a few evenings. Recommended with cozy mode on for players who dislike horror.

**Not recommended for:** players wanting depth, replayability, or a long game; players sensitive to jumpscares or motion sickness (no FOV slider); and anyone who bounced off the demo or *A Game About Digging a Hole*, since it is the same experience with more collectibles. Many reviews explicitly say **wait for a sale** (~$1–3).

## Caveats

- This document summarizes player opinion; it is not a technical review. Issues reported in reviews (crashes, achievements, autosave) may have been patched since the review was written — several reviewers note the game was receiving updates and ask for FOV/cozy options that have since been added.
- Steam review totals change over time; the sample is the complete set of 330 reviews visible via the API on 14 September 2026.
- Quotes were lightly cleaned (line breaks, censored profanity) and translated from German, Russian, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Czech where necessary. Attribution gives language, playtime at review, and sentiment.
